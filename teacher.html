TideLine · Teacher page
Play TideLine

TideLine is a cosy 3D fishing game on the Illawarra coast for NSW Aquatic Technology 7–10, option Aquatic harvesting (Stage 5, Module 2). Students cast, land a fish, name it with a dichotomous key, measure it and choose: keep it or release the small. Nothing is uploaded: each student’s Fish Guide and logbook stay on their own device.

At a glance

25species in the Illawarra Fish Guide
5sites: beach and rocks, lake, harbour, offshore reef, river
3–4lessons (about 50 minutes each)
0accounts, uploads or ads
  • Devices. Chrome or Edge on a school Chromebook or laptop (keyboard and mouse), or a phone or tablet held sideways (touch controls). It needs WebGL 2. The first load downloads about 5 MB (3D models and scripts), then the browser caches it; another site adds a little more. For slow Chromebooks add ?quality=low (or choose Low in Settings).
  • The game spine. Each fish is Seen → Caught → Identified (the key) → Mastered (a measure within 1 cm and the right keep-or-release call). The tagline: “Gotta catch ’em all… and release the small.”
  • Endings. Every fish Caught: a club celebration. Every fish Mastered and Bay Health at 71% or more: awards night at the Illawarra Anglers’ Club, then the credits. After either the student keeps playing. Nell (Belmore Basin) then offers the commercial co-op licence chapter.
  • Money. Recreational fishers cannot sell their catch, so Club credits are paid only for good science and good care (records, tags, comps, missions, litter). The only place a fish is ever sold is the co-op, with the commercial licence, and only a commercial haul.

A 3–4 lesson sequence

Students work alone or in pairs. Each lesson ends with one “show me” from the logbook or Fish Guide. Use the deep links below to start every student in the right place; ?play=1 skips the title screen and sends them straight to the water.

Lesson 1 · about 50 min

Rules on the beach: measure, bag and release

  • Start: Bellambi beach and rock platform (?eco=rocky&play=1). Meet Sione (rock safety), Bev (the club) and the Fisheries Officer.
  • Do: cast, land 5 fish, measure each on the brag mat, decide keep or release; talk to the Officer on the way home.
  • Look for: students who measure to the nearest cm; “one cm under is still undersize”; why a slot limit protects big breeders.
  • Show me: open the logbook (L): which fish were legal to keep, and why not for the others?
Lesson 2 · about 50 min

The lake: name it, then master it

  • Start: Lake Illawarra (?eco=seagrass&play=1), a rising tide in the morning (&tide=rising&time=dawn). Meet Mitra (licence, fee, bait limits), Old Tam and Dr Priya (tag and release).
  • Do: solve the dichotomous key for two species; Master one; collect bait inside the DPIRD limits; tag and release a legal fish.
  • Show me: the Fish Guide (J): one Mastered card with its stock status and sustainable swap.
Lesson 3 · about 50 min

Technology, safety and pests: harbour, reef and river

  • Start: Belmore Basin (?eco=harbour&play=1&time=dusk, opens at 5 species Identified; the deep link opens it anyway). Squid jigging and the wharf lamps; the offshore reef (?eco=reef&play=1) with the fish finder; the freshwater river (?eco=river&play=1&flow=high).
  • Do: read the fish finder (arches, clouds, the thick bottom line); the lifejacket and safety beat; the river’s flow instead of tide; the bass closed season (&season=winter) and the carp “best practice” card.
  • Show me: what is the difference between a rule (closed season) and best practice (pest fish)?
Lesson 4 (extension) · about 50 min

Awards night and the commercial chapter

  • Start: students who have reached 100% Caught see the club celebration; those who have Mastered everything see awards night (?ceremony=awards previews it for the class). Then Nell’s co-op licence at Belmore Basin.
  • Do: in the Nets lab choose a gear, a mesh size, a bycatch reduction device (BRD) and a turtle excluder (TED) and meet the lab goals; land a haul and sell it at the co-op; read the stock status and the sustainable swaps.
  • Show me: compare the recreational and commercial table in the Sell tab. Why can an esky full of legal fish never be sold, even with a licence?

Deep links and QR codes

Add these to the web address (after index.html, joined with &) to start every student in the same place.

&eco=The site: rocky (Bellambi beach and rocks), seagrass (Lake Illawarra), harbour (Belmore Basin), reef (offshore reef), river (freshwater river). A deep link opens a site even before students have unlocked it.
&season=summer, autumn, winter or spring. winter closes keeping of Australian bass (May to August) on the river. &month=6 sets a month (1–12).
&time=dawn, day, dusk or night (or an hour such as 18.5). Every site has a sunset and a dark-blue night; squid bite only at dusk and night.
&tide=rising, high, falling or low. Sea sites only: the river has no tide.
&flow=low, normal or high (after rain). River only.
&swell=Swell in metres. A swell of 2.5 m or more washes the rock platform and the harbour breakwall (a good safety lesson).

Progress export (logbook CSV)

Everything is saved on the device in the browser’s local storage: the Fish Guide, the logbook, the esky, credits and the angler. Nothing leaves the device, so there is nothing to log in to, and a shared Chromebook keeps one student’s game per browser profile (use New game on the title screen to start again).

  • Logbook (CSV). Press L (the 📒 button on a phone) then Export logbook (CSV). One row per fish, which opens in Sheets or Excel for the numeracy tasks. Columns: angler, date, spot, tide or river flow, time of day, species, length cm, your measure cm, accurate, kept or released, legal to keep, why not, rig, bait. “Accurate” is a measure within 1 cm of the real length.
  • Fish Guide (CSV). The Fish Guide (J) exports one row per species with its tier, longest catch, accurate measures and correct decisions.
  • Class data idea. Collect the CSVs into one sheet: which species were most often undersize? What is the average measuring error? Which spot gave the most legal fish?
  • Printing. This page prints cleanly: use your browser’s Print (the QR codes and links are kept).

Data sources and caveats

Data sources

  • NSW DPIRD (Department of Primary Industries and Regional Development, dpird.nsw.gov.au): size, bag and possession limits, the recreational fishing fee and exemptions, closed seasons, Lake Illawarra closures, Bushrangers Bay, the catch-and-release page and Wollongong Council’s rock fishing page. Limits were checked on 31 October to 1 November 2026. Limits change: check FishSmart or the DPIRD website before using the numbers in class.
  • Module 2 teacher worksheets: the most authoritative source for rules, the Fish Guide hints, the quiz bank and the commercial fishing content.
  • SAFS (Status of Australian Fish Stocks, fish.gov.au, 6th edition, 2024): the stock status of each species. Stock status is by region: a species can be sustainable in NSW and depleted elsewhere.
  • GoodFish (Australian Marine Conservation Society): TideLine points to GoodFish and the FishSmart app and does not copy their colour ratings, because the ratings vary by state and change over time.

Caveats: please read before a class uses it

  • Sea Country content pending AEO/AECG review. The Sea Country ranger’s dialogue, the six Sea Country story cards and the cultural fishing quiz use only worksheet-sourced material (nothing marked [unverified]) and describe practices as living knowledge. They have not yet been checked by the school’s Aboriginal Education Officer (AEO) or the local Aboriginal Education Consultative Group (AECG). Please have them checked, or switch the ranger off by not talking to her, before a class uses them.
  • Carp wording. The game teaches “best practice”, not law: DPIRD encourages keeping and humanely disposing of carp and never moving live carp. Returning a carp is not taught as an offence, because the dataset’s source for this is a search summary and other states differ. Please check the DPIRD pest fish page. The redfin perch rule (live possession) is not taught for the same reason.
  • Unverified biology. Rig, bait, tide, time, season, typical length, fight strength and the identification features are general angler and biology knowledge, not checked against a source. They are gameplay tuning and may differ from real fishing. The river flow preference (bass and eels feed harder after rain) is the same kind of tuning.
  • The nets lab and the co-op use game numbers. The worksheets give levels (seabed High or Low; bycatch Low, Medium to High, High) and mesh definitions, not percentages, prices or quotas. The haul percentages, prices, quota sizes and the rule that the open gap is half the stretched mesh are made up for the simulation, and the screen says so.
  • Quotas and ITQs. The worksheets define a quota as a limit per year. Individual transferable quotas are not in the dataset, so the game does not teach them.
  • Release survival ranges are from DPIRD (for example bream 72 to 97%, snapper 67 to 92%); the game starts from the middle of each range. A deeply hooked bream whose line is cut (12% to over 85%) is a separate lesson.
  • Stock status moves. Mulloway are “recovering” in the 6th edition (older editions said “depleted”) and jackass morwong (Eastern stock) are “depleted”. Some DPIRD pages cannot be fetched automatically, so a few statements are tagged as read from a search summary in the content dataset; they are listed on the content file for checking.
  • Syllabus codes. The syllabus map above uses worksheet numbers and topic wording; outcome codes are not given. Please confirm them against the NESA document.

Progress is stored in the browser’s local storage on each device and is never uploaded. Clearing the browser’s site data, or a different browser profile, starts a new game.
