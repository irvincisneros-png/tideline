## 3. Patterns that work

1. **Visible fish you can target** (ACNH, OoT/TP, FFXV, Cat Goes Fishing, Sea Fantasy, RDR2 ripples). Aversa: visible luring trades surprise for relaxation + skill; OoT is "praised highly" precisely because you see the fish follow and decide. Nintendo's own ACNH guidance is "land the bobber in front of the fish".
2. **Rhythm, not speed, as the presentation skill** (TP "wiggle, pause, wiggle; constant wiggling spooks", RDR2 "small movements attract, press twice scares", Fishing Planet Stop&Go recognised when your reel cadence matches). This maps onto real angling (strikes come on the pause) and is teachable in one sentence.
3. **One readable rule for the fight**: SoT "pull opposite, reel when it stops"; RDR2 "never reel while it thrashes"; I Just Wanted to Fish "let it run, reel while it rests". Players describe all three as intuitive. Multi-sensory wrong-way feedback (rumble, splinter sound, rod bend) makes it learnable without a tutorial.
4. **Forgiving failure gradient** (Dredge: miss = slower, never a hard fail; Webfishing: red bar chases you; Stardew meter drains rather than snaps). Dredge's team made "fishing should not be frustrating" a pillar after finding Stardew too finicky.
5. **Species as motion profiles** (Stardew's five types, Moonglow's four rod counters, CotW's per-species navigation/fight AI). TideLine already has this.

## 4. Patterns that annoy

1. **Pure RNG waits with no agency** (Stardew's 0.6–30 s, Webfishing "click and wait", Minecraft/Pokémon called dull in the taxonomy). The Game Dev's Guide literally writes "waiting, waiting, waiting".
2. **Button-mash and QTE** (Dave the Diver's random keys "clash with chill vibe"; Nice Day for Fishing's mash+parry called repetitive; Monster Hunter mash).
3. **Opaque tension/drag numerics** (CotW "sometimes you can't adjust tension and the line breaks"; RF4/Fishing Planet require forum lore about drag notches vs line rating). Sim depth without on-screen cause-and-effect reads as unfair.
4. **Precision-hungry inputs on touch** (Stardew mobile bar "less precise than keyboard"; RDR2 stick-rotation reel needed an accessibility patch; Fishing Clash offers a Button mode because the slider is "more precision").
5. **Visual direction ambiguity** (SoT: fish looks like it turned before it actually pulls; Abiotic Factor thread: locked camera hides fish direction). Direction-based fights need an explicit indicator, not animation reading.

## 5. Hybrid proposals for TideLine

All three keep: species fight profiles/power, gear-match zone size + jitter, bait vs lure in tackle box, structure/turn timers, litter in the bar, legal-length measuring afterwards.

### A. "Work the lure" rhythm retrieve (Zelda TP / RDR2 model)
- **Loop**: cast as now → if a **lure** is tied on, a retrieve phase starts. Player reels slowly; tapping gives a twitch, holding reels; releasing pauses. A nearby fish (spawned from the species table for that spot/tide) is rendered as a shadow that **follows the lure** when the cadence matches its data-driven preference (e.g. flathead = twitch-pause, tailor = fast roll, bream = slow drag with long pauses). Bite triggers on a pause after a matching sequence. Bait keeps the current passive wait.
- **Keyboard**: Space tap = twitch, Space hold = reel, nothing = pause. **Touch**: same on one thumb anywhere on screen (tap/hold/release). No new buttons.
- **Learn in a minute**: first lure tooltip "Twitch it. Pause. Watch the shadow." The shadow's interest ring fills green on matching cadence and fades on wrong cadence (replaces TP's invisible spook rule with visible feedback).
- **Species shows up**: preferred cadence, follow distance, spook threshold, strike-on-pause vs strike-on-move all per species; fight unchanged.
- **Failure**: constant reeling spooks (shadow turns away, small fish swap in); lure reaches shore → recast; snag chance on reef when paused too long near structure.
- **Complexity**: **medium**. One extra state machine, one shadow mesh + simple steering toward lure, an "interest" scalar; reuses existing cast/strike/fight.

### B. "Let it run" tension fight replacing the vertical bar (SoT / RDR2 / IJWTF model)
- **Loop**: cast/bite as now; fight becomes a tension gauge with a safe band. Fish alternates **run** (power × profile) and **rest**. Holding reel during a run drives tension up; releasing lets it run and tension falls; reel during rest brings fish closer. Direction optional: an arrow shows run direction; tapping the opposite arrow key (A/D) eases tension faster (bonus, not required).
- **Keyboard**: Space hold = reel; A/D = lean rod opposite. **Touch**: hold thumb = reel; drag thumb left/right = lean. Single contact.
- **Learn**: "Reel when it rests, let go when it runs." Rod bend + line hum + colour band give three redundant cues.
- **Species**: dart = short rapid runs; bolt = one long run; jet = runs that ramp; twitch = tiny runs that tempt you to over-reel; structure line = run heads toward reef, lean away to steer; turn timer = diving fish needs you to lean up. Gear match widens the safe band and slows tension rise.
- **Failure**: tension pegged for 1 s = line pop; slack too long = hook throws (meter drains); structure reached = bust-off.
- **Complexity**: **medium-large**: replaces a working minigame, retunes every species, redo litter-catch and tutorials. Highest payoff in feel but riskiest for a live game.

### C. "Sink-and-stop" depth presentation (Cat Goes Fishing / sinking-lure model), minimal change
- **Loop**: after the float lands, player can hold Space to **slowly draw the rig toward shore**, and release to let it sink/settle; a depth/distance readout plus visible seafloor/reef shows where it sits. Fish density per species is a data field (surface / mid / bottom / near structure). Bite timer multiplier comes from matching the rig's resting zone; a brief shadow flicker signals "something is near". Lures get an extra multiplier when moved in short pulls.
- **Keyboard**: Space hold = draw in, release = settle. **Touch**: thumb hold/release.
- **Learn**: "Fish live somewhere. Put the bait there." Tackle box hint lists each species' zone once it has been caught.
- **Species**: zone and bait preference drive wait time; fight unchanged.
- **Failure**: resting on reef with a plain rig = snag (lose rig); drawing in too fast with bait = no bites; surface-only presentation never reaches bottom species.
- **Complexity**: **small**: one hold-to-move on the existing float, a zone multiplier in the existing bite timer, a snag check.

### Recommendation: **A** (rhythm retrieve), with C's "draw toward shore" as its idle behaviour.

Why: it is the only option that adds the thing players consistently praise and TideLine lacks — a visible fish reacting to what you do before the bite (Zelda, ACNH, FFXV, Cat Goes Fishing) — while leaving the Stardew-style fight, species profiles, gear match and litter untouched. It reuses Space/one-thumb tap-hold-release, so Chromebook and phone inputs stay identical. It turns the current RNG wait into a short skill loop that a Year 7 can describe in a sentence ("twitch, pause, watch"), it gives lures a reason to exist, and it fits the marine-science framing (prey mimicry, species feeding behaviour, ambush vs cruising predators) better than a tension sim. B is the better fight but the current bar is already liked and B's tension numerics are the most common complaint in sims; keep B as a later "legendary fish" mode. C alone is too passive to change the feel but is cheap enough to fold into A as what happens between twitches.
